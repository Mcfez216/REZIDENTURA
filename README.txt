REZIDENTURA
A KGB case officer's career, 1968-1991. Windows 10 / 11, 64-bit.

TO INSTALL
  Run Rezidentura-Setup.exe. It installs for your Windows account only, so it asks for no
  administrator rights, and puts Rezidentura in the Start menu (and on the desktop, if you
  tick it). Remove it from Settings > Apps, or with "Uninstall Rezidentura" in the Start menu.
  Your saved games are kept in your "Saved Games" folder (C:\Users\<you>\Saved Games\
  Rezidentura) and survive reinstalling; the uninstaller asks before deleting them.

  Windows may say "Windows protected your PC", because the installer is not signed with a
  paid certificate. Click "More info", then "Run anyway".

  It needs DirectX 11, which every Windows 10 / 11 PC has. On a machine with no graphics
  card (a virtual machine, a remote desktop) it falls back to Windows' software renderer.
  For a first game, tick the training course when you begin: the instructor teaches one
  exercise a day.

TO PLAY WITHOUT INSTALLING
  Rezidentura.exe also runs straight from this folder: keep manual.txt, "Officer's
  Handbook.pdf" and the "fonts" folder beside it. While the file portable.txt is here, saved games go into a "saves"
  folder beside the program; delete portable.txt to use your Saved Games folder instead.

THE CAREER
  June 1968  Moscow ......... the training course (optional)
  1968-1971  London ......... case officer
  1973-1977  Vienna or New Delhi ........ senior case officer
  1979-1984  Washington or East Berlin .. head of line
  1986-1991  Bonn or East Berlin ........ Rezident / head of line
  August 1991, Moscow.
  Where you go next depends on how the last tour went. Being expelled ends a posting, not
  the game: the career goes on (up to a point), but some capitals are closed to you.

WHAT YOU MANAGE
  - Your days: three blocks a day, planned five weeks ahead.
  - People whose real qualities you never see: spotting, assessing, developing, recruiting.
  - Agents: tempo, tasking, pay, cameras and film, their own tradecraft, their nerves, and an
    investigation at their office that you only hear about second-hand.
  - Communications plans: meeting rotations, fall-backs, dead drops, signal sites, call-outs,
    one-way radio with one-time pads.
  - Surveillance you can only infer: short checks, prepared detection routes that wear out,
    countersurveillance teams, abort policies, burned and staked-out sites.
  - Provocations: dangles, walk-ins, fabricators, agents who are caught and turned.
  - The chain to Moscow: cassette -> drop or meeting -> lab -> Thursday pouch -> evaluation.
  - Cipher traffic rationed in groups a day; stores with lead times; money to the last pound.
  - Service A (active measures) and Line N (illegals support) tasks.
  - Other officers: who has seen which agent and which site; defectors; a possible traitor in
    the station; the marked-card test; reports to Directorate K.
  - Your own officers: assignments, cases, orders, and their mistakes.
  - Officers of foreign services as recruits: what they tell you about your own affairs,
    and whether to believe it.
  - Exfiltration of blown agents, listening devices, brush passes.
  - How you get to each act: on foot, the Embassy car, or a taxi.
  - A card index of the watchers' cars: plates you know, plates they change.
  - Agents' own lives: promotions, illness, polygraphs, postings abroad.
  - Monthly returns for the whole career, drawn as charts.
  - Each city's weather, daylight and summer time; host-country holidays; transport strikes.
  - Safe flats rented through cut-outs, documents photographed overnight, nosy concierges.
  - Centre's verification of agents with control questions.
  - The operational game: playing an approach back at the other side under Line KR.
  - Agents' skills you train: security, photography, elicitation, talent-spotting, comms.
  - Events: delegations, defections, audits, journalists, influenza, flash requirements...
  - A city that changes: venues close and open, building works, new observation posts.
  - The yearly work plan and the December attestation.
  - Home and leave: your wife's patience, thirty days in the Union, her help with signals.
  - Your own money, things for Moscow, a protector at Centre - and keeping back agents' pay.
  - Compromise operations against contacts, and what the other side may hold against you.
  - The military neighbours (GRU), rival colleagues, provocations at the Embassy gate.
  - Chicken feed, and playing a controlled agent back at the people who control him.
  - Confidential contacts; writing up the newspapers as a source (and being caught at it).
  - The anonymous letter through the door: the best agent you will ever have, or bait.
  - Standing escape plans: a carrier bag at the signal site, a bar of chocolate in answer.
  - The summons to Moscow.
  - The real calendar of 1968-1991 in the background.

REFINEMENTS SINCE 1.6
  - Exfiltrations now count: each agent brought out is entered on the posting record and
    in Centre's evaluation of the tour, and the first earns a new citation, "Nobody left behind".
    (Before, the count was kept but never used.)
  - The planner's inks: street work in red pencil, detection routes in blue, and a "!" on any
    meeting or drop that has no detection route before it. Hover an order for what it does.
  - Agreed meetings open with the usual agenda filled in (pay owed, film, camera, instruction),
    and a button puts it back.
  - The checklist also notices: acts with no detection route before them, agents with no film
    or no camera, cables stuck in the cipher queue, and the next step with a developing
    contact (trace him; then propose him to Centre).

NEW IN 1.6
  - A new look: the desk is now dark, with every screen in a brass-framed panel with its
    own title bar. Paper is kept for what really is paper - files, cables, reports, orders.
  - Icons on the screen list, the buttons and the figures at the top; a date plaque that
    turns over when the day ends; dense, easier-to-read registers and tables.
  - New typefaces for the desk (Oswald for titles and labels, PT Sans for text), a little
    larger than before. The typewriter is unchanged.
  - The launcher, the dialogues, the settings, the notices after each day and the windows
    lifted off the desk all wear the same frames.

NEW IN 1.5
  - The game opens in a small launcher window and grows into the desk when a career begins.
  - A cleaner desk: plain English names for the screens, grouped; a status card with the
    figures that matter; the typewriter kept for documents and a clear typeface for the rest.
  - Begin a career at any posting (London 1968 to Bonn 1986), with one of four backgrounds,
    and play it as an ironman career if you like.
  - Every Rezident runs his station his own way - and you have to find out which.
  - Service records across careers: fifteen citations to earn and seven endings to find.
  - Short notices after each day; routine cables filed quietly; numbered answers to decisions.

THE DESK
  - Any screen can be opened in a window of its own (right-click its name),
    dragged to a second monitor, or dropped on another window to share a frame with it.
    Settings has "Reset windows" and "Close all windows".
  - Settings (the Menu button at the top right, or F10): windows, full screen (F11), the
    size of everything, what stops "Skip ahead", confirmations, backups.
  - A checklist of what wants doing, made fresh each morning (F2), and a notebook.
  - Quick save and quick load (F5 / F9); the last three month-end autosaves are kept.
  - Keys: Ctrl+Enter ends the day, 1-9 and 0 change screen, F1 opens the handbook.
  - Orders that repeat; a search box on the card index; your own notes on a man's file;
    new codenames for agents; your personal file written out as text.
  - The Officer's Handbook as a PDF made up as a registry file (also in the Start menu),
    and on the Handbook screen with a list of sections and a search.

TO CHANGE THE GAME
  The full source is in this folder. Edit, then run build.bat (Visual Studio or MinGW-w64),
  or use CMake (CMakeLists.txt).

    src\content.cpp ..... the course and the seven postings: districts, map, streets, names,
                          jobs, requirements, historical events. The easiest place to start.
    src\core.h .......... the data only: people, sites, film, orders, officers, the game state,
                          and the save-file format. Fields marked HIDDEN are never shown to the player.
    src\sim_day.cpp ..... the planner, staff actions, and everything that happens in the street.
    src\sim_night.cpp ... your officers' day, the night, the dawn, Centre's answers, history.
    src\sim_career.cpp .. generators, starting and ending a posting, the years at Centre, decisions.
    src\sim_spy.cpp ..... officers of foreign services, exfiltration, listening devices.
    src\sim_tutorial.cpp  the training course.
    src\sim_life.cpp .... the watchers' cars, getting about, agents' own lives, monthly returns.
    src\sim_world.cpp ... weather and daylight, holidays, safe flats, verification, the operational game.
    src\sim_events.cpp .. the events deck, the changing city, agents' skills and talent-spotting.
    src\sim_office.cpp .. the plan and attestation, home and leave, your own money, compromise operations.
    src\sim_trade.cpp ... chicken feed and playback, confidential contacts, walk-in letters, escape plans.
    src\sim_text.cpp .... the texts the screens show (dossier, stores, station, accounts ...).
    src\art.cpp ......... the photographs, drawn by the program (faces, streets, paper, wood).
    src\gfx.cpp ......... paper, stamps, seals, pencil, typewriter, the emblem, the objects in the safe.
    src\emblem.h ........ the hammer and sickle, as an embedded image.
    src\skin.cpp ........ the chrome: brass-framed panels, title bars, buttons, the icons.
    src\ui.cpp .......... the frame, the header and the screen list, popups, the order form.
    src\ui_tabs.cpp ..... the desk screens, the map and the documents (Dear ImGui).
    src\main_win32.cpp .. the Windows window and DirectX 11.
    src\rezidentura.rc .. the icon (rezidentura.ico) and version information.
    installer\ ......... the installer script (NSIS 3) and its pictures. build.bat builds the
                          installer too if NSIS (nsis.sourceforge.io) is installed.
    src\ui_extra.cpp .... windows lifted off the desk, settings, keys, checklist, handbook screen.
    src\ui_launch.cpp ... the launcher: continue, a new career, saved games, service records.
    src\records.h ....... the citations and endings kept across careers.
    manual.txt .......... the handbook.
    tools\make_handbook.py  sets manual.txt as "Officer's Handbook.pdf" (Python with
                          reportlab and Pillow). Run it after changing the manual.

  Two test programs build on any system (build_test.sh on Linux, or the CMake targets):
    bot ......... plays whole careers with no screen and prints how they went. Run it after
                  changing a rule:   bot 200 careful      bot 200 reckless
    uitest ...... runs the real screens with no window, clicks through them by script, and
                  writes PNG screenshots:   uitest <output folder>

  Saved games from versions 1.1, 1.2, 1.3, 1.4 and 1.5 load in this version (1.6). Older
  ones cannot be loaded.

  third_party\imgui is Dear ImGui 1.91.5 (the "docking" branch) by Omar Cornut and contributors (MIT licence, see
  its LICENSE.txt).

  The typefaces in "fonts" are under the SIL Open Font License (the OFL-*.txt files):
  IBM Plex Mono (the typewriter text), Oswald (titles and labels), PT Sans and PT Sans
  Caption (the rest of the desk), PT Sans Narrow and PT Serif (ParaType), Russo One,
  Stalinist One, and Bad Script (pencil).

All people in the game are invented, and every photograph is drawn by the program.
The places and the dates are real.
